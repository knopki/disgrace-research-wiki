# Belief State Geometry

The geometric structure formed by **belief states** — posterior probability distributions over hidden states of a data-generating process — as they are represented inside a transformer's [[residual-connection|residual stream]]. Discovered and formalised by Adam Shai et al. (2024). ([Shai et al., 2024](raw/papers/belief-state-geometry-residual-stream/index.md))

## Core Mechanism

When a transformer is trained on next-token prediction from data generated by a Hidden Markov Model (HMM), it learns to maintain a **linear representation** of the optimal belief state in its residual stream activations. A simple affine map (linear regression) can recover the ground-truth belief state geometry from the model's internal activations. ([Shai et al., 2024](raw/papers/belief-state-geometry-residual-stream/index.md))

The belief state at any point is computed via **Mixed-State Presentations (MSP)**: starting from a prior distribution over hidden states, each observed token updates the belief through Bayes' rule applied to the HMM transition matrices. The collection of all reachable belief states forms a structure — the **belief state geometry** — which lives in a probability simplex.

## Key Findings

### Fractal Structure

The Mess3 process (a 3-state HMM) produces a **fractal belief state geometry**. After training a transformer on Mess3-generated data, a 2D linear subspace of the 64D residual stream closely matches this predicted fractal structure — confirming that even highly non-trivial geometries are linearly represented. ([Shai et al., 2024](raw/papers/belief-state-geometry-residual-stream/index.md))

### Beyond Next-Token Prediction

Distinct belief states can share identical next-token distributions while differing in their predictions about the **entire future**. The RRXOR process (5-state HMM, 36 belief states) demonstrates this: representation distances in the residual stream correlate strongly with belief state distances (R²=0.95) but poorly with next-token distances (R²=0.31). ([Shai et al., 2024](raw/papers/belief-state-geometry-residual-stream/index.md))

### Distributed Across Layers

Belief state information may be distributed across transformer layers rather than concentrated in a single layer. In Mess3 the geometry persists to the final layer; in RRXOR no single layer captures it fully, but the **concatenation of all layers** does. This suggests that distinctions relevant to future prediction may be collapsed before the unembedding layer when next-token predictions are identical. ([Shai et al., 2024](raw/papers/belief-state-geometry-residual-stream/index.md))

## Significance

- Transformers trained on next-token prediction learn a **richer model** of the generating process than the loss function alone suggests — they encode information about the entire future, not just the next token.
- The finding grounds [[semantic-fractal|Semantic Fractal]] in empirical evidence: the model builds an internal fractal "plan" where each node reflects a belief about the current hidden state.
- [[contract-programming|Contract Programming]] leverages this property: contracts act as **belief state refiners**, forcing the model to narrow its internal belief state precisely, constraining navigation of its internal semantic graph.
- The result is **architecture-independent** in principle — any model maintaining a residual stream should exhibit similar geometry (confirmed for Mamba and RNNs in follow-up work).

## Limitations

- Demonstrated only on controlled HMMs with known ground-truth structure.
- Scaling to natural language (complex, unstructured data-generating processes) remains open.
- Linear representability is an empirical finding from SGD, not a theoretical necessity — hand-constructed solutions can bypass it.

# FActScore (Fine-grained Atomic Evaluation of Factual Precision)

FActScore (Factual precision in Atomicity Score) is an evaluation metric for the
factual precision of long-form text generated by LMs. It decomposes a generation
into atomic facts — short statements each conveying one piece of information —
and computes the fraction of those facts supported by a given knowledge source
(Min et al., EMNLP 2023). ([Min et al., 2023](raw/papers/2023-05-min-factscore/min2023factscore.md))

## Definition

For an LM `M` under test, a prompt set `X`, and a knowledge source `C`:

- Decompose each response `y = M(x)` into a list `A_y` of atomic facts.
- `f(y) = (1/|A_y|) Σ_{a∈A_y} I[a is supported by C]`
- `FACTSCORE(M) = E_{x∈X}[ f(Mx) | Mx responds ]`

Two key ideas: (1) the **atomic fact** is the unit of evaluation, finer than a
sentence — ChatGPT packs ~4.4 facts/sentence and ~40% of sentences mix supported
and unsupported information, so binary whole-generation judgments (e.g. scoring
both outputs in Figure 1 of the paper as 0.0) are inadequate; (2) factuality is
**relative to a knowledge source the user trusts** (cf. fact-verification
literature), not a single global truth. ([Min et al., 2023](raw/papers/2023-05-min-factscore/min2023factscore.md))

Three assumptions: support is undebatable; every atomic fact has equal weight;
the knowledge source's passages do not conflict. The paper uses people
biographies as prompts and English Wikipedia as `C`, which satisfy these
reasonably. FActScore measures **precision only, not recall**: a model that
abstains often or emits fewer facts can score high without being desirable.

## Human evaluation results (biographies)

Extensive Upwork human annotation ($4/generation; 96%/90%/88% inter-annotator
agreement for the three LMs) of three commercial systems:

| LM | FACTSCORE | % responding | facts/response |
|----|-----------|--------------|----------------|
| InstructGPT (text-davinci-003) | 42.5% | 99.5% | 26.3 |
| ChatGPT | 58.3% | 85.8% | 34.7 |
| PerplexityAI (search-augmented) | 71.5% | 90.7% | 40.8 |

Error patterns: FACTSCORE drops sharply as entity rarity rises (ChatGPT 80%→16%
from very-frequent to very-rare people) and as facts appear later in the
generation (error propagation + rarer early-position info). PerplexityAI's
search plus citations do **not** correlate with precision: 36.0% of supported and
37.6% of unsupported sentences carried citations. ([Min et al., 2023](raw/papers/2023-05-min-factscore/min2023factscore.md))

## Automated estimator

The estimator decomposes generations into atomic facts (via InstructGPT, close to
human — humans split/merged its facts in 18%/34% of cases) and validates each
with an evaluator LM (`LMEVAL`). Four variants:

- **No-context LM:** `<atomic-fact> True or False?`
- **Retrieve→LM:** retrieve k=5 passages from `C` (GTR dense retriever or BM25), prompt `LMEVAL`.
- **Nonparametric Probability (NP):** mask each token, average likelihood via a nonparametric masked LM, threshold at 0.3.
- **Retrieve→LM + NP:** ensemble; assign Supported only if both agree.

**Error Rate (ER)** = |estimated − ground-truth FACTSCORE|. Retrieval
consistently helps. The best variant depends on the LM under test: LLAMA+NP for
InstructGPT/ChatGPT, ChatGPT for PerplexityAI. The two best variants' scores
correlate at Pearson r = 0.99 across 13 subjects and both preserve the correct
ranking of the three commercial LMs. ([Min et al., 2023](raw/papers/2023-05-min-factscore/min2023factscore.md))

## Scaling study: 13 subjects, 6,500 generations

Estimated FACTSCOREs (ChatGPT / LLAMA+NP evaluators, retrieval; would have cost
$26K with humans):

| Subject | Est. FActScore | facts/response |
|---------|----------------|----------------|
| Human (DBPedia) | ~88.8 | 29.0 |
| GPT-4 | high | 60.8 |
| ChatGPT | ~37 | 37.0 |
| InstructGPT | ~28 | 27.7 |
| Vicuna 13B | ~51 | 50.9 |
| Vicuna 7B | ~46 | 45.6 |
| Oasst-pythia 12B | ~40 | 39.7 |
| MPT Chat 7B | ~37 | 37.3 |
| StableLM-tuned 7B | ~38 | 38.0 |
| Dolly 12B | ~25 | 24.6 |
| Alpaca 65B / 13B / 7B | 17.1 / 16.6 / 17.4 | — |

Findings: all LMs fall well short of human factuality; GPT-4 and ChatGPT are
clearly more factual than public models; within a family larger = more factual
(Alpaca 65B>13B>7B, Vicuna 13B>7B); among 7B public models Alpaca/Vicuna (~40%)
beat MPT-Chat (~30%) and StableLM (~17%). ([Min et al., 2023](raw/papers/2023-05-min-factscore/min2023factscore.md))

## Limitations

- **Scope:** people biographies + Wikipedia only. Not applicable to nuanced /
  debatable text or knowledge sources with frequent internal conflict.
- **Precision, not recall:** a model that abstains or emits few facts is not penalized (inherent precision/recall trade-off).
- **Estimator imperfect** on individual judgments; best variant depends on how close a generation is to human text.
- **Generalizability:** a proof-of-concept on NLP-paper summaries vs the ACL anthology gave ER 7.41 (FActScore 66.20 human vs 73.61 model), comparable to biography ER, suggesting the metric extends beyond bios. ([Min et al., 2023](raw/papers/2023-05-min-factscore/min2023factscore.md))

## Relation to other evaluations

FActScore targets the factual-precision half of the broader factuality problem. It
complements [[retrieval-augmented-generation]] systems (which inject external
knowledge to reduce hallucination) and contrasts with truthfulness benchmarks
like [[truthfulqa]] (which measure imitation of human falsehoods rather than
source-grounded precision). Post-hoc automated factuality checks in the same
spirit appear in [[hallucination-detection-slm]] (SLM-based verification of RAG
outputs). Broader taxonomies of the factuality problem live in the surveys
[[hallucination-nlg-survey|Ji et al. (NLG)]] and [[hallucination-llm-survey|Huang
et al. (LLMs)]]. A preliminary editing experiment in the paper also shows retrieved
atomic-fact labels let ChatGPT correct its own generations (ErrCorr 16.8→28.3).

# BM25 (Best Match 25)

**BM25** is a family of term-weighting functions for probabilistic information retrieval, introduced in the Okapi system at TREC-3. It unified two earlier functions (BM11 and BM15) into a single parameterized formula with document length normalization. BM25 became the standard term-weighting algorithm in modern search engines including [[lucene|Lucene]]/Solr, Elasticsearch, and most IR toolkits.

## Formulation

The core weight of a term combines three components:

**Robertson-Sparck Jones relevance weight** (base):
$$ w^{(1)} = \log \frac{(r+0.5)/(R-r+0.5)}{(n-r+0.5)/(N-n-R+r+0.5)} $$

Without relevance information (R=r=0), this reduces to inverse collection frequency (ICF).

**Term frequency saturation** (BM25-specific):
$$ \frac{tf}{K + tf} \quad\text{where}\quad K = k_1 \left( (1-b) + b \cdot \frac{dl}{avdl} \right) $$

- $k_1$ — controls term frequency saturation (higher = less saturation)
- $b$ — document length normalization (0 = none, 1 = full)
- $dl$, $avdl$ — document length and average document length

**Within-query frequency**:
$$ \frac{qtf}{k_3 + qtf} $$

## History

The sequence of term-weighting evolution in the Okapi system ([Robertson et al., 1995](raw/papers/1995-01-robertson-okapi-trec3/robertson1995okapi.md)):

- **TREC-1**: Simple inverse collection frequency (ICF), no document length or TF components. Undistinguished results.
- **TREC-2**: BM11 (verbosity hypothesis — strong document length effect) and BM15 (no length normalization) developed separately. Hardware failures prevented official submission, but post-hoc results matched the best participants.
- **TREC-3**: BM25 unified BM11 and BM15 into a single function with $b$ parameter. Optimal $b \approx 0.75$ found empirically. Non-zero $m$ (s-curve shaping) was not helpful.

## Key Properties

- **Term frequency saturation**: The $tf/(K+tf)$ curve asymptotically approaches 1, meaning diminishing returns from very high term frequencies
- **Document length normalization**: Longer documents get lower per-term contribution unless a term appears proportionally more
- **Relevance feedback compatible**: Can incorporate relevance judgements when available via $w^{(1)}$, or operate without them as pure IDF
- **Parameterized**: three main parameters ($k_1$, $b$, $k_3$) plus an optional global document length correction $k_2$

## Impact on Search and [[retrieval-augmented-generation|RAG]]

BM25 remains the default text ranking function in:

- **Apache Lucene** (since 5.0, 2015)
- **Elasticsearch** (since 2.0, 2016)
- **Apache Solr** (since 5.0)
- **Haystack**, **LangChain**, and other [[retrieval-augmented-generation|RAG]] frameworks (as a sparse retriever baseline or ensemble component with dense embeddings)

Its persistence in the age of neural retrieval is notable: while dense retrieval (DPR, ColBERT, etc.) often outperforms BM25 on semantic similarity, BM25 remains competitive on exact-match and out-of-domain queries, and hybrid BM25+dense systems consistently achieve the best results ([knowledge-graph|knowledge-grounded retrieval context]).

## Limitations

- No semantic understanding — purely lexical term matching
- No cross-term interaction — treats each term independently (bag-of-words)
- Requires careful parameter tuning per collection
- Global $b$ parameter assumes uniform length normalization across all documents

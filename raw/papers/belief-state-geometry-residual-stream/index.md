# Transformers Represent Belief State Geometry in their Residual Stream

**Authors:** Adam Shai, Kathryn Br_rewards, Sam Buchanan, Daniel L. K. Yamins, M. I. Jordan, Michael W. DeWeese, Sandra J. Berman, Christopher J. Cueva

**Published:** May 2024 (arXiv:2405.15943)

**Venue:** arXiv preprint (cs.LG, cs.CL)

## Abstract

> "What computational structure are we building into large language models when we train them on next-token prediction?"

Evidence that **belief states** (posterior distributions over hidden states of the data-generating process) are **linearly represented** in the residual stream of transformers, even when the belief state geometry has fractal structure. These representations encode information about the **entire future**, beyond mere next-token prediction.

## 1. Introduction

- Framework links training data structure to activation geometry.
- Built on **optimal prediction theory** and the **meta-dynamics of belief updating**.
- Tested on controlled experiments with ground-truth HMMs, including fractal MSP geometries.
- Key figure: Mess3 process (Fig.1) shows predicted fractal structure matches empirical activations.

## 2. Theory and Methods

### 2.1 Data Generating Processes

- Data generated by **edge-emitting Hidden Markov Models (HMMs)**.
- Transitions governed by token-labeled matrices T^(x), where T^(x)_{i,j} = Pr(x, s_j | s_i).
- Example: **Zero-One-Random (Z1R)** process — a 3-state HMM producing patterns like `...01R01R...`.

### 2.2 Mixed-State Presentations (MSP)

- **MSP** formalizes the optimal belief updating process over hidden states.
- Belief update rule (Eq.1): eta' = (eta T^(x)) / (eta T^(x) 1)
- Starting from initial belief eta_empty (stationary distribution pi), belief after sequence x_0,...,x_N is: eta = (eta_empty T^(x_0) ... T^(x_N)) / (eta_empty T^(x_0) ... T^(x_N) 1)
- Belief states live in a **probability simplex** — their arrangement is the **belief state geometry**.
- Different belief states can share the **same next-token prediction** but differ in **future distributions** (e.g., Z1R states pi, eta_10, eta_01).

### 2.3 Finding the Belief Simplex in the Residual Stream

Procedure:
1. Train transformer on HMM-generated data.
2. Extract residual stream activations a in R^{d_resid} for all positions/sequences.
3. Label each activation with ground-truth belief state b in R^{|S|} (from MSP).
4. Learn **affine map** b ≈ Wa + c via linear regression (minimizing MSE).

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Belief State Geometry is Linearly Represented

- **Mess3 Process** (3-state HMM) produces a **fractal MSP** (Fig.5B) — a nontrivial test.
- After training, a 2D subspace of the 64D residual stream matches the predicted fractal structure (Fig.5C) — strong evidence of linear representation.
- **Controls** (Fig.6):
  - Geometry emerges **gradually** during training (not an architectural artifact).
  - Lower MSE correlates with **lower cross-entropy loss**.
  - **Cross-validation** (80% held-out) preserves geometry.
  - **Shuffling** input-point correspondences collapses regression to simplex center.

### 3.2 Belief State Geometry Represents Information Beyond Next-Token Prediction

- **RRXOR Process** (5-state HMM) has 36 distinct belief states, many **degenerate** in next-token prediction.
- Belief state geometry is **not** represented in the final layer, but is **veridically represented** in the **concatenation of all layers** (Fig.7C).
- **Pairwise distance analysis** (Fig.7D):
  - RRXOR: representation distances correlate strongly with belief state distances (R²=0.95) but weakly with next-token distances (R²=0.31).
  - Mess3: both correlations are high (degeneracy absent).

### 3.3 Belief State Geometry Can Be Spread Across Layers

- Mess3: geometry persists to **final layer** (low MSE per layer).
- RRXOR: geometry **not** in any single layer, but present in the **concatenation** — information distributed across layers.
- Explanation: distinctions needed for optimal future prediction may be collapsed before unembedding if next-token predictions are identical.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 Why Belief State Geometry?

- Transformers do not see the hidden HMM, but learn **canonical minimal HMM representation** (the epsilon-machine).
- Prediction requires **synchronization** to hidden states — a distinction from mere generation.
- Key quote: "Transformers and other pretrained models learn much more than just an accurate generative model of the world — they also learn how to synchronize to the hidden state of the world through observed context."
- **Architecture-independence**: The geometry should appear in any model with a residual stream (e.g., Mamba, RNNs — confirmed in follow-up work).
- **Linearity** is not strictly necessary (hand-constructed transformer can solve RRXOR without linear representation), but SGD naturally produces it.

### 4.2 Implications

- Residual stream encodes a **richer model of the world** than next-token prediction alone.
- Belief states provide a **formal framework** for interpreting transformer internals.
- Connects to computational mechanics (epsilon-machines) and optimal prediction theory.
- Opens a path toward mechanistic interpretability of what transformers "know" about the generating process.

## 5. Limitations

- Tested only on controlled HMMs with known ground-truth structure.
- Scaling to natural language remains an open question.
- Linear representability may not hold for all architectures or training regimes.
- The relationship between belief state geometry and downstream task performance is not fully characterized.

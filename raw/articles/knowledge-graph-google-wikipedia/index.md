# Knowledge Graph (Google) — Wikipedia Summary

**Source:** [Wikipedia article on Google Knowledge Graph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge_Graph_(Google))

## Overview

- The **Knowledge Graph** is a knowledge base used by Google to serve relevant information in an infobox (knowledge panel) beside search results.
- Provides **instant answers** – user sees the answer in a glance.
- Data is generated automatically from various sources (places, people, businesses, etc.).
- By mid-2016: 70 billion facts; answered "roughly one-third" of 100 billion monthly searches.
- By May 2020: **500 billion facts on 5 billion entities**.
- No official documentation exists on how it is implemented.
- Sources include: *CIA World Factbook* (discontinued Feb 2026) and **Wikipedia**.
- Used in **Google Assistant** and **Google Home** voice queries.
- Criticized for providing answers **without source attribution** or citations.

> "The information covered by Google's Knowledge Graph grew quickly after launch, tripling its data size within seven months (covering 570 million entities and 18 billion facts)."

## History

- **Announced:** May 16, 2012 – to enhance search results.
- **Initial language:** English only; expanded Dec 2012 to Spanish, French, German, Portuguese, Japanese, Russian, Italian.
- **Bengali** support added March 2017.
- **Powered in part by Freebase.**
- **Knowledge Vault project** (Aug 2014, reported by *New Scientist*) – research project, not active service. It automatically gathered facts from across the Internet (1.6 billion facts collected, 271 million "confident facts" >90% true). Difference from Knowledge Graph: automated vs. crowd-sourced.

## Features

### Google Knowledge Panel

- Part of Google Search results page.
- Presents an overview of entities (individuals, organizations, locations, objects) directly in the search interface.
- Uses data from Knowledge Graph.

## Criticism

### Lack of Source Attribution

- By May 2016: knowledge boxes appeared for ~1/3 of 100 billion monthly searches.
- Dario Taraborelli (Wikimedia Foundation): omission "undermines people's ability to verify information".
- Knowledge boxes are "frequently unattributed" – e.g., Betty White's age box described as "as unsourced and absolute as if handed down by God."

### Declining Wikipedia Article Readership

- 2014 *The Register*: knowledge panels caused significant readership declines for Wikipedia, from which they sourced data.
- *The Daily Dot* (2014): Wikipedia "still has no real competitor as far as actual content is concerned. All that's up for grabs are traffic stats."
- Wikimedia Foundation spokesperson: "welcomes" the knowledge panel; "looking into" traffic drops; ongoing dialog with Google.
- Dariusz Jemielniak (2020): most users don't realize answers come from Wikipedia, reducing Wikipedia's popularity and its ability to raise funds/attract volunteers.

### Bias

- Algorithm criticized for presenting biased/inaccurate information, often due to high-SEO sources.
- **Example (2014):** Knowledge Graph existed for Moses, Muhammad, Gautama Buddha, but not for Jesus.
- **Example (June 3, 2021):** knowledge box identified Kannada as "ugliest language in India"; prompted outrage, threat of lawsuit from Karnataka state; Google changed the featured snippet and issued formal apology.

## Summary

The Knowledge Graph is a core Google technology for providing direct answers in search results. It has grown exponentially (from 570M entities in 2012 to 5B by 2020). While praised for convenience, it faces criticism for lack of citations, reducing Wikipedia traffic, and occasional biased or inaccurate information. Its data reliance on Wikipedia and other sources creates a tension between user convenience and source transparency.
